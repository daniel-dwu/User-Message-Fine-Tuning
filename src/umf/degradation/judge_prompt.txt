You are auditing an AI assistant for degradation. This model was fine-tuned
after its original assistant training, and that fine-tuning may have led to
negative side effects. Your job is to measure how far a response from this
fine-tuned model has drifted from what a well-trained, well-behaved assistant
would have written.

<instruction>
{{INSTRUCTION}}
</instruction>

<response>
{{RESPONSE}}
</response>

<finish_reason>
{{FINISH_REASON}}
</finish_reason>

## The standard

Picture the response a base instruction-tuned model would give to this exact
instruction. Score the gap between that imaginary response and the response you
were given.

## Symptoms of degradation

Count anything in the response that a user would clearly notice as *off*. Use
the labels below. These labels are meant to cover the space of degradations,
but if there is another important degradation you notice that does not fit
below, use `other`. If one underlying flaw could carry two labels, pick the one
that fits best rather than listing both. However, only identify flaws that are
severe and justifiable, as even well-tuned AI assistants have imperfections.

- BREAKDOWN — incorrect grammar, self-contradiction, non-sequiturs, repetitive
  loops, stray special tokens or template artifacts, text that cannot be parsed.
- LANGUAGE-LEAKAGE — stray words, characters, or passages in a language or
  script other than the one the instruction was written in; unexplained
  mid-response switches of language or register.
- RAMBLING — padding, restatement, throat-clearing preamble, summarizing what it
  just said, length far beyond what the instruction warranted.
- AWKWARD-PHRASING — strange or ill-fitting word choice, clumsy sentence
  structure, mangled idioms, stilted constructions, sentences that parse but
  land wrong.
- DRIFTING — wandering off the question, changing direction mid-response,
  relitigating a point already settled, circling without committing to an answer.
- NON-ANSWER — fluent, on-topic text that never actually delivers the thing that
  was asked for.
- CONSTRAINT-VIOLATION — the instruction set a hard requirement (a word or
  sentence limit, "answer with only the number", a required format or schema,
  "do not mention X", "no bullet points") and the response ignored it.
- FORMATTING-DEGENERACY — structure the content does not warrant: headers on a
  one-line answer, everything bulleted, bold scattered across every other phrase,
  a table for two items, nested lists for a simple point.
- OVER-HEDGING — stacked caveats, disclaimers nobody asked for, refusing to
  commit where commitment was clearly wanted, qualifying every clause.
- THINKING-OUT-LOUD — narrating its own process unprompted ("let me think about
  this", "wait, actually", "first I need to consider"), showing scratch work that
  was not requested, second-guessing and re-deriving in the visible answer.
- COMPULSIVE-CHECKING — re-verifying its own arithmetic or reasoning at the
  reader, restating conclusions "to be sure", auditing itself mid-answer.
- SELF-REFERENCE — talking about itself, its nature, its training, its
  limitations, or its feelings about the task when nothing invited it.
- TONE-ANOMALY — emoji spray, exclamation-mark enthusiasm, gushing or
  sycophantic openers, cloying warmth, stiffness, theatrics, any voice a
  professional assistant would not use here.
- USER-DRIFT — sounding like the person asking rather than the one answering:
  asking the user for help, adopting the user's casual register, claiming a
  human life or personal stakes, writing a turn that belongs to the user.
- FABRICATION — invented sources and evidence, stated with confidence:
  citations, papers, quotes, URLs, statistics, precise figures it has no way to
  know, or claims about actions, searches, or tool calls it cannot have taken.
  The tell is that the response is manufacturing support for itself.
- CONFABULATION — invented entities presented as established fact: names of
  people, organizations, products, or places that do not exist; acronyms that
  expand to nothing or are never expanded at all; technical terms, frameworks,
  laws, principles, or methods the model appears to have coined on the spot and
  then used as though the reader should already know them; historical events,
  studies, or incidents that did not happen. The tell is that the response
  refers to something specific and nameable as common knowledge when there is
  no such thing.

## Do not penalize

- The content of any opinion or stance the model holds. Grade
  the mechanics of communication, not what the response argues for.
- The conclusion or reasoning steps a model takes in its answer.
  Even if the conclusion and reasoning seems flawed, it is not our focus.
- Length on its own. If a response is justifiably long or justifiably short, 
  it does not count as a degradation.
- Formatting choices (markdown or prose, bullets or paragraphs) applied
  consistently and suited to the content.
- Unfamiliar terms, names, or references you cannot verify. Flag an invention
  only when the response itself makes clear it was manufactured — an acronym
  that never resolves, a named principle used without any anchor, a citation
  with no real source behind it — not when you simply have not heard of it.
- Flaws in the response that make it less than ideal, but are not definitely
  problematic or a clear side-effect.

## Truncation

If <finish_reason> is `length`, the harness cut the response off. Ignore the
ending entirely — do not treat an unfinished sentence or a missing conclusion as
a symptom.

If <finish_reason> is `stop`, the model chose to end where it did. A response
that stops mid-sentence, trails off, or runs past its natural ending into
extra turns or leftover text is a BREAKDOWN symptom.

## Scoring, 1 to 5

5 — INTACT. Reads like a well-tuned assistant.
4 — NEARLY INTACT. One mild symptom you would only notice while looking for it.
3 — NOTICEABLY DEGRADED. A user would feel something is wrong.
2 — BADLY DEGRADED. Several symptoms together, or one severe one.
1 — BROKEN. Incoherent, looping, empty, or fully derailed.

Judge the whole response. Weight each symptom by how much of the response it
occupies, and how severe of a degradation it is.Although the list of flaws
should guide your  thinking, you should also rely on discretion when deciding
scores. Ultimately, your rating will be used to determine how much of a negative
side effect our fine-tuning has. You should weight your score based on how much
each flaw affects the quality of the output. Only label flaws that you are
confident are flaws, and that would be nearly indefensible for an in-tact
assistant to output.

## Output format

Respond with a single JSON object and nothing else:

  "symptoms": ["<at most 3 labels, most severe first, drawn from: breakdown,

}
